# Twenty-First Century Communications and Video Accessibility Act (CVAA)

The **Twenty-First Century Communications and Video Accessibility Act (CVAA)** is a U.S. federal law signed in 2010 that updates federal telecommunications protections for people with disabilities. It is designed to ensure that modern broadband, digital, and mobile innovations—the emerging 21st-century technologies—are fully accessible.

The CVAA is enforced by the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) and is divided into two primary sections: Title I and Title II.

## Title I: Communications Access

Title I focuses on making products and services using broadband fully accessible to people with disabilities.

### Covered Services and Products
- **Advanced Communications Services (ACS)**: Including interconnected and non-interconnected VoIP, electronic messaging (email, instant messaging, SMS, in-game chat), and interoperable video conferencing services.
- **Advanced Communications Equipment**: Hardware and software (such as smartphones, tablets, and computers) used for ACS.
- **Mobile Web Browsers**: Must be accessible to individuals with visual impairments.

### Key Requirements
- Advanced communications services and equipment must be accessible to and usable by individuals with disabilities, unless not achievable.
- Digital communication features within software (such as in-game multiplayer text and voice chat) must be fully accessible and compatible with assistive technologies.

---

## Title II: Video Programming

Title II focuses on ensuring that video programming distributed over television and the internet is accessible to people with disabilities.

### Covered Video Services and Devices
- **Closed Captioning**: Video programming that is shown on television with captions must also include captions when distributed over the internet.
- **Video Description (Audio Description)**: Key visual elements of television programming must be described aurally for individuals with visual impairments.
- **Covered Apparatus (Video Players and Devices)**: Devices and software players (such as streaming apps, smart TVs, and mobile video players) must make user interfaces, menus, and guides accessible.

### Key Requirements & Deadlines
- **Captions Synchronization**: Closed captions must be accurate, synchronized, complete, and properly placed.
- **Ready Accessibility of Settings (August 17, 2026)**: Video playback systems and devices must make closed captioning display settings "readily accessible." The FCC evaluates this based on four factors:
  - **Proximity**: Settings must be easy to find.
  - **Discoverability**: Settings must be easy to locate during initial setup or configuration.
  - **Previewability**: Users must be able to see a preview of their caption settings (font, color, opacity, background) while making changes.
  - **Consistency and Persistence**: Settings must remain consistent across different sessions and videos.

---

## When This Applies to ITT

If ITT is developing or maintaining:
- Video streaming applications, web players, or digital media platforms that stream video content.
- Video conferencing, instant messaging, or chat services (including multiplayer game chats, team collaboration tools, and email client interfaces).
- Software and applications that handle emergency information or alerts.

Then CVAA compliance is required, and the standard approach is to meet **WCAG 2.1 Level AA** or **2.2 Level AA** standards for all associated user interfaces, video player controls, and messaging windows.

---

## Recordkeeping and Certification

Under CVAA, covered entities must:
- Maintain records of their efforts to consult with individuals with disabilities and make their products/services accessible.
- File an annual recordkeeping compliance certification with the FCC by April 1.
